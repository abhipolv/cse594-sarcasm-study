Is this tweet sarcastic?

A short CSE 594 study of how people judge sarcasm. You'll read short tweets. About 3–5 minutes, no time limit.

Your task

Decide whether each tweet's author meant it sarcastically: they meant something different from (often the opposite of) what the words literally say. Irony, exaggeration, understatement, and rhetorical questions count when the author doesn't mean the literal statement.

  • The correct answers come from the tweets' own authors, so judge what the author meant.
  • Many tweets are by UK users about politics, football, and TV. If you don't know a reference, give your best guess.
  • Unsure? Pick a "Probably…" answer.
  • Please don't look things up or use other AI tools. One item checks that you're reading carefully.

Examples

Love that someone broke into my car this morningSarcastic
What a horrible day 😔Not sarcastic
I am so ridiculously proud of the job I do!💜Not sarcastic

This version

You will judge each tweet on your own. No AI suggestions are shown.

Each tweet comes with a suggestion from an AI model: its answer, its confidence (50–100), and a short reason. The AI can be wrong. The final answer is yours.

Content note: some tweets contain strong language or mention politics or COVID-19.

About this study and your data
  • This is a class study for CSE 594 (Human-AI Interaction) comparing how well people judge sarcasm with and without AI suggestions.
  • Tweets come from the public iSarcasmEval research dataset (SemEval 2022).
  • We record your uniqname, your answers (including any you change), how long each answer takes, whether you leave the page during a tweet, and any comments. Nothing else.
  • Only the student running the study and the course staff see the data, and it is used only for this assignment.
  • Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any time by closing the page.

By starting, you agree to take part in this study.
